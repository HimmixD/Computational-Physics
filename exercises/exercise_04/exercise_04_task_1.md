# Exercise 4 - Task 1: Symplectic integrators for the Moon

The calculations and figures are generated by
[`exercise_04_moon.py`](exercise_04_moon.py).  The reusable numerical
methods are in [`ode_methods.py`](../ode_methods.py).

## 1a - Circular-orbit initial conditions

With the Earth at the origin,

$$
\ddot{\mathbf r}=-\frac{\mu}{|\mathbf r|^3}\mathbf r,
\qquad \mu=GM.
$$

For a circular orbit, the centripetal acceleration equals the gravitational
acceleration:

$$
\frac{v_M^2}{r_M}=\frac{GM}{r_M^2},
\qquad
v_M=\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r_M}}.
$$

Choosing the initial point on the positive x-axis and counter-clockwise
motion gives

$$
\mathbf r(0)=(r_M,0),\qquad
\mathbf v(0)=(0,v_M).
$$

Using $M=5.972\cdot10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $G=6.674\cdot10^{-11}\,
\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$, and $r_M=384400\,\mathrm{km}$:

| quantity | value |
|---|---:|
| $r_M$ | $3.84400\cdot10^8$ m |
| $v_M$ | $1.018266\cdot10^3$ m/s |
| Kepler period $T=2\pi\sqrt{r_M^3/(GM)}$ | $27.452898$ d |
| sidereal month | 27.32 d |

The Kepler period is calculated from the ideal two-body model with the Earth
fixed and the quoted mean Earth-Moon distance.  The sidereal month is measured
relative to the distant stars and the real system is not an ideal fixed-Earth
point-mass problem: the Moon has a nonzero eccentricity, the Earth also orbits
the Earth-Moon barycentre, and solar and planetary perturbations alter the
orbit.  Small differences also result from rounded constants and the choice of
mean radius.

## 1b - Methods and ten-orbit trajectories

The implemented methods are explicit Euler, implicit Euler, Heun, symplectic
Euler (velocity-first), and velocity Verlet.  Implicit Euler solves

$$
\mathbf y_{n+1}=\mathbf y_n+\Delta t\,f(t_{n+1},\mathbf y_{n+1})
$$

with Newton's method.  The initial guess is one explicit-Euler step from
$\mathbf y_n$; the default tolerance is $10^{-12}$ in the infinity norm.
The iteration count is accumulated and printed/available from
`integrate_orbit`.  For the 6 h run, Newton takes typically 3 iterations per
successful step (177 iterations before the inward-collapsing trajectory
reaches the point-mass singularity at about 14.5 d).  This breakdown is
reported as a warning rather than silently treated as a successful orbit.

The result is shown in [`task_1b_orbits.png`](exercise_04_task_1_output/task_1b_orbits.png).
Explicit Euler gains energy and spirals outward.  Implicit Euler loses energy
and spirals inward.  Heun is not symplectic: its small energy error has a
secular drift, so it eventually spirals outward for this problem.  Symplectic
Euler and velocity Verlet keep the energy bounded and therefore keep a
qualitatively bounded orbit.

## 1c - Energy and angular momentum

The specific energy and angular momentum are

$$
E=\frac12|\mathbf v|^2-\frac{GM}{|\mathbf r|},\qquad
\mathbf L=\mathbf r\times\mathbf v.
$$

Since $\dot{\mathbf r}=\mathbf v$ and $\dot{\mathbf v}=-GM\mathbf r/r^3$,

$$
\frac{dE}{dt}=\mathbf v\cdot\dot{\mathbf v}
+\frac{GM}{r^2}\dot r
=-\frac{GM}{r^3}\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf r
+\frac{GM}{r^2}\frac{\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf v}{r}=0.
$$

Likewise,

$$
\frac{d\mathbf L}{dt}
=\dot{\mathbf r}\times\mathbf v+\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf v}
=\mathbf v\times\mathbf v-\frac{GM}{r^3}\mathbf r\times\mathbf r=0.
$$

The 100-orbit relative errors are in
[`task_1c_invariants.png`](exercise_04_task_1_output/task_1c_invariants.png).
Symplectic Euler conserves the scalar angular momentum exactly for this
central-force discretization: its position update is
$\mathbf r_{n+1}=\mathbf r_n+\Delta t\,\mathbf v_{n+1}$ and its velocity
update changes $\mathbf v$ by a vector parallel to $\mathbf r_n$, so
$\mathbf r_{n+1}\times\mathbf v_{n+1}
=\mathbf r_n\times\mathbf v_n$.  The other methods show finite angular
momentum errors.  The bounded
oscillations of symplectic Euler and Verlet are nevertheless much better over
long times than the secular drift of non-symplectic methods.

Heun has order 2, but order is an asymptotic short-time accuracy statement.
It is not symplectic, so small phase and energy errors accumulate coherently
over many orbits.  Symplectic Euler has lower local order but bounded
long-time energy behaviour, which can make it better after 100 orbits.

## 1d - Half-period convergence

The exact result at half a period is $\mathbf r(T/2)=(-r_M,0)$.  The errors
for $\Delta t=T/200,\ldots,T/6400$ and the fitted log-log slopes are stored in
[`task_1d_errors.csv`](exercise_04_task_1_output/task_1d_errors.csv), with the
plot in [`task_1d_convergence.png`](exercise_04_task_1_output/task_1d_convergence.png).
The expected slopes are 1 for both Euler methods, 2 for Heun and symplectic
Euler, and 2 for velocity Verlet.  The measured slopes printed by the script
should approach these values as the steps become smaller.

For a 1000-year lunar simulation I would use velocity Verlet (or another
high-quality symplectic method), because it is second order, time reversible
for this autonomous problem, and has bounded long-time energy error.  A
smaller step than 6 h should be selected after a convergence test; the
decision should be based on the required phase accuracy, not only on bounded
energy.
